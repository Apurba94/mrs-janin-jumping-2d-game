# Mrs Janin — Mobile Runner Design Direction

## Three Possible Directions

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |
|---|---|---:|
| Sun-Stripe Sprint | A bright coastal road race with bold shadows and hand-cut scenic silhouettes. It feels optimistic and fast without becoming visually noisy. | 0.06 |
| Paper-Cut Dash | A theatrical shadow-puppet world where Mrs Janin’s strong silhouette springs through layered, tactile cut-paper obstacles. It prioritizes clean jump readability on a phone. | 0.03 |
| Neon Rooftop Relay | A cool night-time rooftop course lit by small electrical signs and moonlit haze. It frames the runner as a stylish urban endurance test. | 0.08 |

## Chosen Approach: Paper-Cut Dash

### Design Movement
The game uses a **contemporary paper-cut storybook** aesthetic. Layered terrain, visible grain, chunky silhouettes, and limited high-chroma accents make the runner readable at a glance on compact screens.

### Core Principles
Every obstacle must have a recognizable silhouette before it reaches the player. The character stays visually grounded at the left third of the screen, while the path opens to the right as a clear timing lane. Large mission labels, score numerals, and touch areas remain unobstructed and never compete with the playfield.

### Color Philosophy
Indigo dusk establishes the stage; marigold marks momentum and success; coral marks collisions and danger; parchment white carries important text. The palette is a visual language: safe landing surfaces stay warm, obstacles move toward coral, and the active mission uses marigold.

### Layout Paradigm
The playfield fills the screen like a vertically framed story page. A narrow upper “mission ticket” carries the level and goal; the bottom third contains an oversized thumb-friendly jump control and a compact score marker. The character and obstacles occupy the visual center, not a central marketing card.

### Signature Elements
The game repeatedly uses torn-paper edge lines, offset ink shadows, and a small marigold thread motif that traces a successful jump arc. Obstacles are outlined in thick charcoal with a coral paper edge.

### Interaction Philosophy
The fundamental decision is one-tap timing. A player may tap anywhere in the safe playfield or use the large JUMP pad, with keyboard support for desktop testing. A second tap in the air activates the unlockable double jump, but only from Mission 3 onward.

### Animation
Mrs Janin uses a compact squash-and-stretch jump. Obstacles glide at consistent speed, the camera layer drifts subtly, and mission transitions arrive with short upward paper slides. Collisions freeze the runner briefly, then reveal an immediate retry action. All nonessential motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
**Bowlby One SC** creates bold mission labels and playful scores; **DM Mono** provides compact utility text and high clarity for controls. Headline type uses uppercase with loose tracking, while body labels remain short and operational.

### Brand Essence
**Mrs Janin is a one-thumb mobile runner for players who want an approachable jump challenge that matures into a precision test.**

The personality is **spirited, tactile, and determined**.

### Brand Voice
The voice is direct, warm, and game-like. It treats every mission as a small triumph rather than a product instruction. Example lines: “ONE MORE HOP.” and “THE ROAD GETS CLEVER.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a small marigold circular jump arc crossing a charcoal stick-figure silhouette. The wordmark pairs the mark with chunky split-line lettering reading “MRS JANIN”.

### Signature Brand Color
**Janin Marigold — #F7C843** is the ownable progress and action color.

## Style Decisions

The first visible frame is always the actual side-scrolling course with a mission ticket and a huge thumb-reachable jump control. Rounded UI is used only for soft paper tabs and the jump pad; obstacles, terrain, and HUD edges retain a cut-paper angularity. A mission must communicate “what to do next” in fewer than six words.

Onboarding is an in-world mission ticket rather than a centered marketing card, and it never overtakes the runner, timing lane, or jump control. Every first-visible mission state includes a coral obstacle with a charcoal silhouette at the right side of the course. Janin Marigold is repeated as a dashed jump-thread arc that links the runner to a safe trajectory, not merely as a button or score color.
